# Dropship vs. Original R&D: Evidence Matrix

Use this note for prelaunch wearables, cameras, gadgets, and other branded consumer electronics where the storefront may obscure the product's actual engineering provenance.

## Fast evidence matrix

| Evidence | What it supports | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Exact chassis sold under many brands | Private-label/OEM reuse | That the seller contributes no software, QA, or support |
| No exact reverse-image match | Possible custom tooling or unreleased product | Original electronics, algorithms, or patents |
| FCC filing in seller's name | Real regulatory integration and accountable grantee | In-house PCB design or sensor science |
| FCC internal photos and manuals | Physical hardware exists; model/chip/antenna clues | Production quality or measurement accuracy |
| Product-specific App Store seller and frequent updates | Sustained software/product operation | In-house code; it may be commissioned or ODM-derived |
| Generic companion app shared by many brands | Commodity platform | No customization at all |
| Prior shipped product with owner reports | Company is more than a one-page preorder funnel | New product readiness |
| Patents/publications with matching mechanism | Potentially original technical work | Commercial implementation or superiority |
| Only renders/composites | Product maturity is unproven | Fraud or nonexistence |
| Missing component-level specifications | Marketing maturity exceeds technical disclosure | Automatically bad hardware |
| Health claims without validation | High accuracy/medical-risk uncertainty | That every wellness metric is useless |

## Search workflow

1. Open the supplied source directly and save exact product language, dimensions, model names, images, manuals, and promised ship dates.
2. Search exact quoted phrases and dimensions; search model numbers, Bluetooth names, charger geometry, and sensor layout.
3. Reverse-search several independent views: hero front, case back/sensors, side/crown, charger, and app screenshots. A single hero image is too easy to crop or rerender.
4. Search FCC/device-report databases by brand, legal company, model, grantee code, and support-email domain. Inspect internal/external photos and test reports when available.
5. Check Apple/Google app listings for legal seller, privacy-policy domain, release cadence, languages, ratings, firmware support, and whether the same app serves unrelated brands.
6. Search Alibaba, Global Sources, Amazon, manuals sites, import records, and other brands for exact hardware—not merely broadly similar styling.
7. Inspect company identity: legal entity, addresses, prior products, team/about material, warranty/returns, support history, and policy-template residue.
8. Separate current evidence from preorder promises and marketing copy.

## Layered classification

Assess each layer independently:

- **Industrial design:** stock enclosure, cosmetic customization, or new tooling?
- **Electronics:** standard module/reference PCB, modified ODM board, or seller-designed architecture?
- **Sensors:** named commodity parts, custom packaging, or genuinely novel sensing?
- **Firmware:** generic vendor stack, customized firmware, or proprietary embedded platform?
- **App/cloud:** shared white-label app, branded fork, commissioned product app, or mature proprietary service?
- **Algorithms:** vendor defaults, presentation-layer scoring, validated proprietary models, or unknown?
- **Validation/regulatory:** generic declarations, seller-owned filings, independent performance studies, medical clearance?

A common and useful conclusion is: **custom industrial design plus ODM electronics and a commissioned app**. This is neither literal dropshipping nor deep vertically integrated R&D.

## Wording guardrails

- Say “I found no public filing under the searched brand/model” rather than “it has no FCC certification.”
- Say “reverse search found no exact clone” rather than “the design is definitely original.”
- Distinguish a legitimate company from a mature or accurate product.
- Do not infer fraud from CGI; renders are normal before production. Treat them as absence of proof of functional hardware.
- Do not treat “Original Equipment” in an FCC application as a claim that every subsystem was designed in-house.
- Describe undefined proprietary claims as **unsubstantiated**, not false, unless contradictory evidence exists.
- For health wearables, distinguish general-wellness disclaimers from validated accuracy and regulatory medical claims.

## Recommended answer shape

1. Direct classification and confidence.
2. Evidence against literal dropshipping.
3. Evidence limiting the R&D claim.
4. Layer-by-layer likely provenance.
5. Purchase threshold: what evidence should appear before preorder or full-price purchase.
