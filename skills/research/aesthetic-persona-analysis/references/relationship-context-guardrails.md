# Relationship-context and scope guardrails

Use these guardrails when a user asks for personality inference about a partner, relative, friend, or unidentified collection owner.

## Ownership and target

- Resolve the short link first: it may point to a profile, a single board, or a single pin.
- Until ownership is disclosed, refer to **the curator**, **the board owner**, or **the projected persona**. Do not assume the user owns the collection.
- State whether the read is profile-wide or board-specific. A fashion board supports conclusions about desired self-presentation more strongly than conclusions about domestic habits, work, or relationships.

## Keep aesthetics separate from functioning

A moodboard can support claims about aspiration, taste, identity performance, and desired emotional atmosphere. It does **not** establish:

- practical competence or independent-living ability;
- family enmeshment or parental control;
- relationship readiness;
- actual spending, possessions, wardrobe, or home;
- psychiatric diagnosis, trauma history, or attachment style.

If later biographical facts are supplied, analyze them independently. Prefer demonstrated behavior over proxies such as mileage from family, job prestige, living arrangement, or visual style. Update the hypothesis promptly when counterevidence appears rather than defending the initial narrative.

## Relationship advice

- Do not pivot from aesthetic interpretation into warning the user that they are “in trouble” unless concrete relationship behavior supports it.
- If the user asks whether a projected trait matters in marriage, translate it into observable questions or behaviors rather than treating the collection as proof.
- Watch for contemptuous framing from either side, but do not use that observation to erase a legitimate asymmetry in experience or responsibility.

## Theme-share estimates

When asked what fraction of a large collection is religious, political, sexual, commercial, or otherwise thematic:

1. Sample visible pins across every accessible board, not just one board.
2. Deduplicate repeated accessibility descriptions.
3. Define **explicitly coded** content separately from merely compatible content (for example, hijab imagery versus generally modest clothing).
4. Report sample size and its share of the total collection.
5. Give a bounded estimate for the whole corpus and state that unloaded/private pins were not inspected.
6. Treat keyword matching as a floor; use screenshots to catch visual content absent from labels.
